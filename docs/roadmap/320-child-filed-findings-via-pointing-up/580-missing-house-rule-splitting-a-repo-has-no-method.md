- [ ] 🎯 **MISSING HOUSE RULE — the house has no method for splitting one repo
      into two, and two children have now each worked one out alone**
      `[M][doctrine]`. Hand-up from a private child via § *Pointing up*,
      2026-10-11. The evidence is held in the children's private records, so
      it is summarised here by shape only. Neither child is named
      (PROPAGATION rule 2).

      ## The gap

      `PROPAGATION.md` covers copying doctrine *down* the tree, and
      `PUBLISHING`-shaped work in the children covers flipping a repo public.
      Nothing in `docs/method/` covers the move in between: **one repo
      becoming two**. That covers tool out of an engagement, data out of a
      product, or a product out of a monorepo. A grep of `docs/method/` for
      *extraction*, *split a repo*, *two repositor* and *fresh export* returns
      no method text (measured at `fd63df8`).

      ## Two instances

      1. **Tool out of a private engagement repo into a new public repo.** It
         ran over about two weeks and is recorded as done. It developed, on
         its own:
         - a routing test (*came from the private side → private; generic
           with synthetic examples → public; both → split, and the motive
           never travels; unsure → private*);
         - a records rule (records never cross, however well cleaned);
         - a multi-layer gate whose first layer is a hash-attested read for
           meaning, with token lists as backstops only;
         - a refuse-first rename script that copies path-keyed assistant
           memory;
         - dated delete-items for every temporary bridge;
         - cold reviews at stage boundaries.
      2. **Estate data out of a product repo into the estate root.** It is
         under way. It started in August, stalled for two months while it
         competed with other work, and on 2026-10-11 was re-planned by
         studying instance 1. Instance 1's lessons transferred almost one to
         one.

      Both children were dragged through the same failures by their
      principal:
      - **keywords instead of meaning**, corrected three times in instance 1;
      - **forking instead of upstreaming**, corrected twice in four minutes;
      - **re-asking a settled ruling** (*"Stop asking, I've ruled before"*);
      - **a guard that skips silently when its data is absent.** Instance 1's
        boundary test did this. Instance 2's tripwire does the same today,
        and was found by the study, before deletion would have exposed it.

      ## Proposal (atelier's to weigh)

      A short `method/` section, *Splitting a repo*, extracted from the two
      instances, not invented. It would cover:
      - the bar and the capability list, both stated up front;
      - the four-line routing test, placed in every onramp that a session on
        either side reads (the user-global one too, when one side may not
        name the other);
      - records never cross;
      - one home per fact, one direction of flow, never fork;
      - a guard built first and proven to fail;
      - migration in measured stages, where a moved result stops the stage;
      - a dated delete-item for every bridge;
      - a refuse-first rename;
      - a handoff file with a *settled — do not re-ask* list.

      The children can supply their records for the extraction on request.
