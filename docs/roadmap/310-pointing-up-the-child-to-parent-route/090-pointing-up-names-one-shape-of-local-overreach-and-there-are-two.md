- [ ] 🔎 **§ *Pointing up* names one shape of deciding a house question locally,
      and there are two — the second one closes the question instead of opening
      it** `[XS][docs]` — raised from faves 2026-08-19 after exercising the
      route cold. Filed rather than patched; see the note at the end, which is
      the part that matters procedurally.
      **The gap.** The section's opening check is aimed at a child that
      *authors* a house rule — reasons from its own block, decides the house has
      a gap, writes the rule locally. The filing child hit that shape and also
      its mirror: **it twice tried to decide a house question locally, once by
      proposing a `[-]` bracket and once by treating the question as already
      settled and closing it.** Same overreach, opposite conclusions, and only
      the first is described.
      🔑 **Why the second is the more dangerous of the two, and the reason this
      is worth a sentence rather than a shrug.** An invented rule is *visible* —
      it sits in the onramp as text a reviewer can find and challenge, which is
      how the original instance was caught within a day.
      🔴 **And a wrongly-closed question is worse than an absence** — this item
      first said it *"leaves nothing behind at all"*, and the filing child
      corrected that on 2026-08-19, from the instance it lived through. **It
      leaves a citable artefact wearing the principal's authority.** The closure
      is written down as a settled answer, so the next session does not read an
      open question and decline to answer it — **it reads a ruling and cites
      it**, which is exactly what happened on 2026-08-17 when a session quoted
      the capture back at Mike as his own position and argued against a stance
      he had never taken. He corrected it from memory.
      **That is what let it survive four weeks and a self-review**: an absence
      is merely unnoticed, while a wrong artefact is *actively defended* by
      everyone who finds it, because it carries the authority nobody re-checks.
      So the two shapes are one mechanism, not two failures that happen to
      rhyme, and `080` is this shape's worked example rather than a neighbouring
      finding — the misattribution there **is** a house question closed locally,
      and it is the reason the closure was quotable.
      **The live example is in this same section.** `060` records that the
      2026-07-23 *"keep three"* ruling answered whether the **bracket** should
      carry a disposition, and was then read as having refused the broader
      **indexing** question — which was never put. A settled-looking answer to
      a narrower question stalled a live one for four weeks, in the parent's own
      records rather than a child's.
      **Candidate wording, offered not recommended:** the check becomes *is this
      the house's to decide?* — which catches opening and closing symmetrically
      — with the current *don't author it locally* as one of two consequences,
      the other being *don't retire it locally either; a house question is
      closed by the house, and a ruling that looks adjacent is not a ruling on
      your question.*
      ⚠️ **Deliberately NOT patched into the section, and this is a rule-4
      matter rather than caution.** § *Pointing up* is **self-authored doctrine
      under an OPEN cold-pass cycle** — the `⏳` at `310/050` is queued and
      **unrun**. Its author amending it now would hand the reviewer a moving
      delta and would be the author grading their own gap. The AW6 ruling does
      permit touching a queued delta if the pointer's delta list widens in the
