- [ ] 🔎 **Two corroboration failures worth minting, both self-caught by the
      child that made them.** Offered upward by a child session as method
      material rather than as an ask, from an episode where three sessions
      reached a wrong shared conclusion about a doctrine clause. Recorded here
      because the child's own framing is better than a later reconstruction
      would be, and because it asked that the errors stay on the record rather
      than be tidied away.
      **(a) Agreement is not corroboration when the second party never opened
      the source.** One session asserted that a child repo had mistranslated a
      doctrine clause. A second backed the claim and added supporting
      evidence. A third read the doctrine and found both wrong. Two-of-three
      agreement felt like confirmation and was in fact **one unread claim with
      an echo**. [`EVIDENCE.md`](../../method/EVIDENCE.md) already requires
      *"a second angle that can't share the first's"* blind spot; what this
      adds is the operational tell — **when a second voice agrees, ask which
      artefact it opened**, because a peer restating a claim is the cheapest
      possible agreement and the least informative.
      **(b) A symptom count locates a fault's existence, never its site.**
      *"Three independent readers all stalled on this clause"* is strong
      evidence that the deadlock is **real**. It is no evidence at all about
      **which file the defect lives in** — yet it was offered, and received,
      as settling both. That conflation is the sharper of the two, because the
      evidence was genuinely sound and simply answered a different question,
      which is much harder to notice than evidence that is weak.
      **Why this is worth minting rather than filing and forgetting.** Both
      failures are shapes this estate is structurally exposed to: it runs many
      sessions that read each other's records, and its own review doctrine
      leans on independence it mostly cannot verify. The prose scanners cannot
      see either — this is a reasoning defect, not a text defect — so if
      anything catches it, it is framing at the point of use rather than a
      check. That places it on rung 1 of the propagation ladder.
      **Also a live worked example of the class already on the board:** the
      fabricated-quotation finding, where a coined paraphrase in quote marks
      became a citable source for a second item. Same mechanism, different
      surface — a claim gaining authority from repetition rather than from
      anyone re-opening the source. **That is now the second recorded
      instance**, which is what the recurrence rule asks for before a rule is
      minted rather than merely noted.
