## Scope

The intent under review, in the work's own words: the floor's purpose is to
*find every secret, credential, private key and piece of personal data so none
of it reaches a public or insecure place*, and that intent is unreachable in a
detect-and-block-only design. E6a writes the posture into `SECRETS.md`; E6b
adds an advisory tier to `secretscan`; E6c generalises the SF1+SF2 carve-outs;
E6d adds impact as a second axis (escalate-only, repo-declared via the D4
seam, `confidence × impact = severity` computed).

In scope: the intent, its factual grounding, the decisions and assumptions,
the sequencing, and the security/privacy posture of the design itself. Out of
scope (the only narrowing): the build — none exists.

## Load-bearing assumptions to attack (the reviewer's own, first act)

- **A1 — the asymmetry is real and undecided.** `leakscan` states
  over-flag-as-fail-safe in source; `secretscan` states no posture and its
  docstring sells precision; no record decided the difference. All three are
  checkable claims at HEAD — re-run them, don't read them.
- **A2 — the risk ranking.** "A leaked credential is actively exploitable in
  a way an address is not" — but this repo's own `SECRETS.md` doctrine makes
  credentials *re-mintable* (detect→rotate→burn, cost-is-minutes) while a
  leaked address in a public tree is unrotatable. Does the claimed ranking
  survive its own doctrine, and does the conclusion (secretscan should
  over-flag more) survive if the ranking inverts?
- **A3 — `severity` is decorative and is confidence mislabelled.** Verify in
  code: any hit blocks regardless of grade; `gcp-oauth-secret` medium on
  pattern specificity; `stripe-key` grades `sk_live_`/`sk_test_` identically.
- **A4 — the one-dial causal story.** The letters-only `SLUG_RX` narrowing is
  attributed to the missing advisory tier. Is that what the comment and its
  history actually record, or is the causal story retrofitted?
- **A5 — "the blocking set never shrinks, so the gate is not weakened."** The
  item itself asks for this to be tested, not assumed. Attack behaviourally:
  who reads advisory output, on what plane (hook stdout vs CI log), and what
  stops the advisory tier becoming a place where real findings go to be
  ignored — the same "nothing sees it" hole the roadmap already records for
  `--no-verify`?
- **A6 — escalate-only + advisory default, jointly.** A real, high-entropy,
  context-free credential in a repo that has not adopted the D4 seam (today:
  all of them) lands advisory and exits 0. Under the stated intent — *none of
  it reaches a public or insecure place* — is report-and-pass an honest
  posture for the boundary gate, or does the design need a consumer for
  advisory findings before it is safe to widen detection into that tier?
- **A7 — repo-declared impact in public trees.** An impact declaration
  ("hits shaped like X open the whole estate") committed to a public child is
  targeting guidance. Does the design say where declarations live and what
  they may reveal, and does the D4 seam (zero adopters) actually carry this?
- **A8 — "computed" without the computation.** E6d(iii) names the formula
  `confidence × impact = severity` but not the matrix, the impact scale, or
  the default impact for undeclared repos. Left to the tool, the matrix is
  decided at tool altitude — the exact failure shape E6 itself names.
- **A9 — E6c's generalised rule.** "In credential-key context, low character
  variety is not evidence of innocence" — check it covers the two ruled
  shapes without re-flagging the placeholder/example paths that placeholder
  detection and allow-markers already carve out.
- **A10 — one-sided mechanics.** E6a's posture governs both scanners, but all
  mechanics land in `secretscan`; `leakscan` keeps no advisory form *by
  design*. Is that a decided asymmetry (fine) or an unexamined one (the
  failure E6 exists to prevent, recurring one level up)? And do the CI plane
  (`floor` workflows) and the hook plane give the advisory tier the same
  meaning?
- **A11 — blast radius and sequencing.** E6a is doctrine children inherit;
  a wrong posture propagates estate-wide. Do the sub-items state an order
  (posture before mechanism), and does anything below E6a depend on a call
  that is still Mike's?

## Security lens (design altitude, run by hand)

Threats that bear on this work's class: **T1** — the advisory tier as cover:
a real secret graded advisory reaches public history behind a green exit;
**T2** — impact declarations as a published map of what matters most;
**T3** — habituation: advisory volume trains readers to skip the block
output too; **T4** — the posture doc itself, being public, tells an attacker
exactly what the floor will not block. Each is either answered by the design,
found against it, or named residual.

## Findings

- **EI1 (MAJOR) — the advisory tier has no named consumer, and on the hook
  plane its surfacing window is one commit.** E6b routes context-free hits
  to report-and-exit-0. Staged-mode `secretscan` sees a value once — at the
  exact commit that puts it into history — so an advisory finding is one
  easily-missed line at the worst possible moment, then silence (T1); the
  CI full-cover run would repeat it, but only inside a green job's log,
  which nobody opens. This repo already names the class: the `--no-verify`
  item — *"nothing sees it"*. The half of the item's own test that holds:
  the blocking set is unchanged, so the **gate** is not weakened. The half
  that fails: the tier's affirmative claim — detection widened *toward the
  stated intent* — is unbacked while an advisory finding has nowhere to
  land and no one who must act. **Counsel (decision Mike's):** make a named
  advisory consumer a build precondition of E6b — where advisory findings
  surface durably (e.g. tree-wide CI listing plus the hook line), whether
  they require acknowledgement, and what happens to one never acted on.
  Without that ruling, E6b widens detection into a tier that functions as
  T1 cover.
- **EI2 (MODERATE) — impact declarations in public children are targeting
  guidance.** E6d(ii) rides the D4 seam, i.e. `.atelier-floor.json`,
  committed in the child's tree. For a public child, a declaration
  enumerating which home-grown credential shapes open the whole estate is
  reconnaissance — the same reasoning this roadmap already applies to
  naming which private repo holds committed credentials (T2). **Counsel:**
  one line in E6d(ii): estate-detail impact declarations live only in
  private repos or machine-local config; public children declare impact in
  class terms only.
- **EI3 (MODERATE) — "computed" without the computation re-opens the
  tool-altitude hole.** E6d(iii) names `confidence × impact = severity`
  but not the impact scale, the combination matrix, or the default impact
  for undeclared repos — which today is every repo (D4 has zero adopters).
  Left unstated, a build session decides all three in code: exactly the
  failure shape E6 itself names ("a coverage narrowing settled at tool
  altitude"). **Counsel:** write into E6d that the scale, matrix, and
  undeclared-repo default are the principal's rulings at build pickup, not
  the builder's.
- **EI4 (minor) — the narrowing's site is misattributed; a builder aimed at
  `SLUG_RX` misses the gap.** Verified at HEAD: `SLUG_RX` is a
  false-positive *suppressor* whose comment records declining to widen an
  existing gap; the coverage narrowing itself lives in `HIGH_ENTROPY_RX`'s
  mixed-class requirement (`tools/secretscan.py:194-206` — single-case hex,
  which is git SHAs *and* lowercase-hex secrets, excluded from the
  context-free net), with the SF1+SF2 probe already measuring its bite.
  A build session steered by the item's wording could "fix" `SLUG_RX` and
  leave the real gap intact. **Counsel:** name both sites in the item.
- **EI5 (note) — the risk-ranking premise is contestable; the conclusion
  stands on firmer ground the doctrine already owns.** "A leaked credential
  is actively exploitable in a way an address is not" invites the counter
  that credentials are re-mintable (SECRETS.md: burn cost is minutes) while
  personal data is unrotatable — which would rank the postures the way they
  currently sit. The stronger grounding for E6's same conclusion:
  **rotation presupposes detection** — the credential recovery story only
  works if the detector over-flags, so the posture E6a writes holds
  whichever way the ranking argument goes. Recorded so `SECRETS.md` doesn't
  inherit a contestable premise as its foundation.
- **EI6 (note) — planes, ordering, and the decided asymmetry.** (a)
  Advisory semantics differ per plane (hook = staged one-shot; CI =
  full-cover repeating): E6b should say which plane means what. (b) State
  the sequencing — E6a's posture lands before E6b–d build to it. (c)
  `leakscan` keeping no advisory form becomes a *decided* asymmetry only
  once E6a records it — make sure E6a does. (d) T4 (the public posture doc
  maps what the floor won't block) is residual and already true of the
  scanners' public source; named, not actionable.
