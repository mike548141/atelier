- [ ] 🔎 **Hand-up: a cross-estate question gets answered from one repo's
      tree, and "it does not exist" goes back to the principal**
      `[S][doctrine]`. Handed up by a private child's orchestrated queue run
      on 2026-10-17, under `PROPAGATION.md` § *Pointing up*. **Evidence, as
      the child records it** (its own item carries the full incident): the
      principal asked whether an issue he had raised was captured for future
      sessions. A session ran `grep` over `docs/` in its own repo, found
      nothing, and told him the issue existed nowhere and that he should
      re-supply it. It was in fact tracked in a sibling repo's board, and the
      original words sat in that sibling's session transcripts (a broader
      `grep` over the transcript store found them in seconds). The session
      then called it "not lost, just not in the repo", and the principal
      rejected that framing: a record no session will ever look in is lost,
      and downgrading the loss removes the reason to build a remedy.
      **The gap — three parts, none of them a single repo's to fix:**
      1. **No cross-repo findability convention.** An item filed in the right
         repo is invisible from the wrong one, so an estate-wide question is
         answered from one tree with false confidence.
      2. **No rule against a negative wider than its search.** "Not in this
         repo's `docs/`" and "nowhere" are different claims; doctrine and
         memory cover "never generalise a negative from a sample", but not
         the scope of a *where* search specifically.
      3. **Capture at the moment of speaking.** A requirement, issue or
         correction the principal states is written to a findable record in
         the same session, not left in a transcript, which is unsearchable in
         practice and impermanent by design.
      Shapes the child can name but has not evidenced: an estate-wide search
      helper (boards plus records across the operator's repos) cited in the
      toolbox; a line in the ask doctrine that an "it does not exist" answer
      states its search scope. Whether any becomes doctrine, and in which
      shape, is for the ruling round.
