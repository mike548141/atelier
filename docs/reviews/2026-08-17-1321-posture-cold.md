**`docs/method/PRINCIPLES.md` § *10. Posture — strength is what you are free
to do* (new, ~72 lines).** A tenth principle placed after §9 and before the
*Trade-offs* section. It opens by distinguishing itself from the other nine
(*they ask how a thing should be built; this asks what being strong is for*),
answers *freedom of action*, and then carries the principal's own statement
**verbatim as a block quote** — three paragraphs on network posture, secret
rotation, and restore/DR — on the stated ground that *the four cases are the
principle*. It distils them to one line (*engineer for the failure being
cheap, not for the failure being impossible*) and four bullets: recoverability
licenses action; a control that makes you reluctant to act is failing even
while nothing has gone wrong (named as the diagnostic half); prefer the
exposure that tells them nothing; prove the recovery by exercising it. A
flagged paragraph names the **precondition** — *"we will know if something
goes wrong"* — as the leg with the least mechanism behind it, and bounds the
freedom to what exercised detection and recovery will support. A closing
paragraph places the section against §1, §5, and four other method docs
(`SECRETS.md`, `DATA-PROTECTION.md`, `ACCESS.md`, `GUARDS.md`) as the homes of
the individual cases.

**`PRINCIPLES.md` § *Trade-offs* gains a situation test, *Prevention, or cheap
failure?*** — placed beside *Gate sizing* as "the prior question": whether the
failure can be made cheap enough that the control stops being the interesting
part; both answers legitimate, differently costed; prefer cheap failure where
the recovery is buildable, keep prevention where it is not, and **say which
you chose**. Its precedent line cites the same-day ruling.

**`docs/method/GUARDS.md` § *The fourth requirement — declared* (new, ~38
lines)** under the existing three (*narrow, noisy, reasoned*): every guard
declares whether it *makes the failure cheap* or *forbids the act*, in a
two-row table with costs; both legitimate, **not declaring is the defect**;
framed as the standard the registry's `why` field was missing; two limits — a
test arriving after the work is grounds to declare, never to unwire a working
gate on the author's judgement, and "forbids the act" is not a failure grade —
and a note that the pass over the open guard board is queued, not done. The
doc's *Related* list gains a line pointing at §10 and the situation test.

## Scope

Widest the work admits. This delta's subject is **a principle whose grounding
is the principal's own words, made a binding requirement on every guard the
same day**, so the reviewable question is not only whether each paragraph is
well written but whether a *principle* was written or a *quotation with
commentary*; whether the fourth requirement is a requirement in the sense the
first three are (checkable per guard, at a defined moment, with a defined
answer slot) or a stance; and whether the two surfaces agree on what the
declaration is. In scope: whether "declares" names a **surface** — the
registry entry, the `why` field, a new field, a doc line — or leaves the
declaration homeless (`GUARDS.md` § *A rule with no home is not a rule*, on
the same page); whether the two limits are enough to stop the requirement
being read as licence to weaken guards, or too much to let it find anything;
whether the *precondition* paragraph binds (does anything say what a session
does when detection is unproven — stop, declare, or proceed?); whether the
four case-homes named actually carry the case (open `SECRETS.md`,
`DATA-PROTECTION.md`, `ACCESS.md` and say whether each states its case in a
form §10 can point at, or whether §10 now states it for them); whether §10's
relationship to §1 and §5 is placement or overlap; and whether a verbatim
principal quotation in a **public** method doc is the right grounding
mechanism for a principle every adopter inherits.
