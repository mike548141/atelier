## Findings

### F1 — MEDIUM (incentive): "finds nothing ⇒ suspect" is a Goodhart trap, and the doc's own grounding case defeats the heuristic

"What review is not": *"A review that finds nothing on non-trivial work is
itself suspect."* Two failures:

- **Perverse incentive.** A reviewer that knows a clean verdict will be
  distrusted is incentivised to manufacture findings — inflate nits into
  numbered items — to look credible. The metric (finding count) substitutes
  for the goal (scrutiny), and the apex's honesty demand ("the honest verdict
  is clean") is put under pressure by the doctrine itself.
- **The repo's own case shows count is no signal.** The author-briefed REACH
  review produced *five findings* — it passes this sniff test comfortably —
  and was still the failure case the independence section exists to fix. Bad
  reviews can be busy; good reviews can be clean.

**Fix:** replace the count-based suspicion with a show-your-work test: a
verdict is credible when it *demonstrates* scrutiny — load-bearing assumptions
named and attacked, live proofs re-run, siblings checked — regardless of how
many findings survived. A clean verdict without that trail is the suspect one.

### F2 — MEDIUM (coherence): the sanctioned cheapest review shape is the least independent, and the sections never touch

"When to review" sanctions the **inline background agent** spawned by the
building session — the shape the atelier foundation review used. But the spawn
prompt *is* the brief, written by the work's author, mid-build, when its
framing is warmest. That is precisely the cold-context/warm-questions shape the
independence section exists to name — and neither section references the
other. A reader of "When to review" alone gets no signal that the spawn prompt
falls under lifecycle step 1's author-brief exception (scope what the work is;
seed nothing).

**Fix:** one sentence in "When to review": the inline spawn prompt is a brief,
and when the spawning session authored the work, the independence rules bind
it in full — it scopes what the work is and stops.

### F3 — MEDIUM (grounding honesty): the grounding is not just n=1 — it is n=1 *uncontrolled*, and the doc names no strengthening trigger

The section states the REACH case "proved" the gap (twice). Beyond the
overclaim-from-one-case (the prior review's I6, endorsed), the case is
**confounded** — three rival explanations share credit for "eight findings,
zero overlap":

1. **Post-fix text.** The re-review read REACH *after* R1–R5's fixes landed
   ("their fixes are all present in the text I reviewed") — zero overlap with
   the first review's findings is partly guaranteed by the fixes, not the leash.
2. **Adversarial priming.** The re-run was commissioned as deliberately
   hostile; the first review was not. Posture alone moves finding yield.
3. **Reviewer variance.** Two cold reviewers given *identical* briefs
   routinely return substantially disjoint finding sets; disjointness alone
   does not isolate the brief as cause.

The mechanism (framing leaks through the ask) is real and worth encoding — the
first review's misses *do* align suspiciously with the seeded questions' aim.
But the honest register is "one supporting case, plausible mechanism", and per
EVIDENCE §10 a non-static claim should name its refresh event.

**Fix:** soften "proved" (I6's fix), add the n=1 honesty clause, and name the
strengthening trigger: the next author-briefed review that passes clean earns
an un-briefed re-run; each such pair is a data point for or against the rule.

### F4 — LOW/MEDIUM (drift): PROPAGATION restates the review policy more strongly than the canonical source now says

`PROPAGATION.md` (enforcement clause): enforcement is *"independent review, by
the most capable model available."* REVIEW.md at HEAD says capability is *"the
multiplier on top, not the precondition"*, deploys the top tier only for
irreversible/structural work, and lets a mechanical gate hold the floor for
the routine. A reader of PROPAGATION alone inherits the stricter, superseded
rule — the exact two-copies drift EVIDENCE §9 forbids, in the doc that
enforces one-source.

**Fix:** PROPAGATION's clause points without restating: "independent review
(see `REVIEW.md` for who reviews and at what capability)".

### F5 — LOW (internal): rule 2 collides with verifying prior dispositions

The re-run rule and lens 2 make the *record* reviewable — including a prior
review's `[fixed]` claims. But verifying a disposition requires reading the
prior verdict, which rule 2 bars until the reviewer's own findings draft. The
REACH re-review resolved this correctly by accident of sequence (probe first,
reconcile after); the doctrine should say that is the design: disposition
verification lives in the *reconcile* step, after the reviewer's own findings
are committed.

### F6 — LOW (underspecified): the principal-disposition handoff doesn't say whether the author may argue

Rule 3 / step 4: the author *"records the verdict verbatim and applies nothing
on its own; the principal decides."* Unstated: may the author attach a
recommended disposition? Silence invites one of two bad outcomes — author
framing re-entering through the recommendation channel, or raw findings dumped
on the principal with no synthesis (a real cost when the principal's time is
the scarcest resource in the loop).

**Fix:** one clause: the author may state its position per finding, labelled
as the author's position and kept below the reviewer's text; the decision
remains the principal's.

### F7 — EDITORIAL: orphaned sentence in step 4

The disposition step reads "...(see *Independence is more than fresh
context*). Fixes consolidate onto one ROADMAP follow-ups item; then tick..." —
the consolidation sentence belonged to the original step and was stranded
mid-flow by the carve-out's insertion (commit `21b1524`). Re-seat it.
