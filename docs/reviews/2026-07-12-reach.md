# Review — REACH.md (the fetch ladder + the credential boundary)

**Scope:** `docs/method/REACH.md` (session 47, just committed). New `method/`
doctrine elevating two rules out of `instruments/browser-fetch/README.md` into
portable form: (a) the cheapest-first fetch escalation ladder, (b) the
credential boundary recast as a *purpose-of-storage* test. Grounding sources:
the browser-fetch README, the ROADMAP "Fetch escalation ladder" item
(docs/ROADMAP.md:157–201), and the SECRETS/ACCESS family it indexes into.

**The three lenses, instantiated:**

1. *Approach & assumptions* — is the ladder the right abstraction of the built
   instance, and does the purpose-of-storage test cut the credential problem at
   the right joint?
2. *Correctness & quality* — is every generic rung and every store-class
   grounded in decided practice, honest about the partial instance, and free of
   overclaim?
3. *Completeness / harvest* — does it sit cleanly in the SECRETS/ACCESS family
   without contradicting or silently duplicating a sibling (one-fact-one-home)?

**Specific assumptions to attack (the pre-seeded sharp questions):**

- **Q1 — grounded ladder, or invented rungs?** Does the generic 1–6 shape stay
  extracted from the Chrome-only built instance, or does it manufacture rungs /
  claims the instance doesn't have? atelier's rule is extract-from-decided-
  practice, never invent to fill a heading.
- **Q2 — is the join argued or asserted?** "Why the two halves are one doc" —
  is a reader *convinced* the ladder and the boundary belong together, or just
  told so?
- **Q3 — does the purpose test cover the real cases without outlawing intended
  use?** The failure mode the author guarded against: doctrine that forbids the
  estate's own provisioned credential use.
- **Q4 — does it leak nothing person-level?** The personal password manager is
  named as a *class*, not an instance — right line, or does anything imply
  specifics? (Reviewer is barred from `~/.claude/` — judged as a genuine
  outside reader.)
- **Q5 — family consistency.** Does REACH sit beside SECRETS/ACCESS without
  contradiction or silent duplication?

**Reviewer:** cold fresh-context agent (2026-07-12), person-level layer barred
by design, read-only in the repo; fixes applied by the coordinating session.
Review deep, not fast. Verdict below the divider; findings with stable IDs
(R1, R2, …).

---

**VERDICT: PASS-WITH-FINDINGS**

Cold fresh-context review, read-only. The doc is strong and the three sharp
questions all come back green: the ladder is a faithful abstraction of the
built instance with no invented rungs, the two-halves join is genuinely argued
(not asserted), and the purpose-of-storage test covers the estate's real
credential cases without outlawing the provisioned ones. No leak of person-level
detail. Five findings, all **Minor or Low, none blocking** — they sharpen
grounding, terminology, and one genuine seam between the two halves; none of
them unseat the design.

**Q1 — the ladder is grounded; no invented rungs.** REACH's generic 1–6 maps
1:1 onto the README's built ladder (built-in/processed ↔ WebFetch/WebSearch;
raw client ↔ curl; real-engine-disposable ↔ `browser_fetch`; operator-started-
isolated ↔ persistent dedicated profile; operator's-live-session ↔ persistent
everyday session; ask-the-operator ↔ ask-the-operator). Every rung-specific
claim survives cross-check: rung 2 "clears no new *walls* … a different *shape*
of request, not a stronger one" matches the README's "same anti-bot profile as
#1"; rung 3 "beats a bare client because it *is* a real engine" matches. The
honest gap is disclosed exactly where it should be — "What lives elsewhere"
states "today the real-engine rungs are one browser; other engines and an
explicit rung-4/5 split are open work," which is verbatim the ROADMAP's open
items (docs/ROADMAP.md:163–170). The doctrine claims nothing the Chrome-only
instance lacks. This is the honesty bar met. (One soft edge: R4.)

**Q2 — the join is argued, not asserted.** "Why the two halves are one doc"
carries a real mechanism ("They meet at rungs 4–5, where extending reach *is*
riding the principal's authenticated session — so the guardrail and the
escalation are the same event seen twice") plus a concrete split-failure mode
("a reader who has the ladder without the boundary would descend it into the
saved-credential store"). The first clause is the load-bearing argument and it
holds under scrutiny: the boundary is *precisely* what governs behaviour at
rung 5 — ride the existing cookies/logged-in tab (permitted), never reach into
the profile's password store to mint a new login (forbidden). The two genuinely
converge at the same event. Convincing.

**Q3 — the test covers the estate's cases without self-contradiction.** The
purpose-of-storage cut (why was it stored, not where it sits) cleanly partitions
the cases visible in the shareable layer: provisioned stores (minted tokens, the
SECRETS/ACCESS machinery) are in-scope-by-design; personal-convenience stores
(browser logins, the principal's password manager) are off by default; the
principal grants across the line and the grant *moves* the credential into the
provisioned machinery. Critically, the boundary does **not** outlaw the one use
the whole ladder exists to reach — riding the operator's live session at rung 5
is explicitly "fair game." Ladder and boundary are coherent, not at war. One
real seam, though, between the *purpose* test and the *browser-riding* rule —
see **R3**.

**Q4 — no person-level leak.** REACH names "the principal's password manager" as
a class; the ROADMAP's own draft names the instance (`Apple Passwords`, and the
principal's browsers-hold-little practice — docs/ROADMAP.md:190–191) and that
detail is correctly kept *out* of the shareable doc. "Saved over years to ease
the principal's own use" is generic colour, not identifying. As an outside
reader I can resolve none of it to a person, a product, or a household. The bar
holds. (The "keychain / estate registry" wording is an *adopter-referent*
problem, not a leak — R1.)

**Q5 — clean family fit, with one deliberate and welcome join.** REACH doesn't
duplicate SECRETS' rotation model or ACCESS's onboarding sequence; it references
them. The standout is lines 80–85: the credential line "runs where `SECRETS.md`'s
own scope boundary runs … This test is the reciprocal from the agent's side."
That's one-fact-one-home done right — SECRETS owns *which credentials the estate
covers*; REACH adds the reciprocal *which the agent declines to draw on*, and
names the shared vault as the single object both halves circle. The "what lives
elsewhere" section mirrors the SECRETS/ACCESS shape faithfully. No silent
duplication found.

**Findings**

- **R1 [Minor] — "the estate registry" is a definite reference an adopter can't
  resolve; "keychain" is ungrounded in the shareable layer.** Line 57 cites "the
  keychain items **the estate registry** records" as a provisioned-store example.
  In the instance that definite article points at shed's registry
  (docs/sessions/2026-07-12-45…:53, "shed registry keychain items") — a concrete
  instrument an outside adopter has no referent for; and "keychain" as a store
  type appears nowhere else in `method/` (SECRETS' model is the encrypted
  sops/age store, not a keychain). REACH is shareable doctrine; a definite "the
  estate registry" reads as if the reader shares a component they don't have.
  *Fix:* make it an explicit illustration with an indefinite article — e.g. "a
  provisioning registry's entries, per-consumer minted API tokens, the whole
  SECRETS/ACCESS machinery" — so the class is clear without gesturing at an
  instance-local mechanism the reader can't see. Push the concrete "estate
  registry / keychain" naming down into the instance's own doc where line 106–107
  already sends it.

- **R2 [Minor] — operator/principal used interchangeably, and the join silently
  depends on them being the same human.** The ladder section says "operator"
  (rungs 4–6, "the operator's attention"); the boundary and the join say
  "principal" ("the principal's saved credentials," "riding the principal's
  authenticated session"). They are plainly the same person here, but the doc
  never equates them — and the Q2 join *requires* it: the operator whose session
  is ridden at rungs 4–5 must be the principal whose vault the boundary guards,
  or "the same event seen twice" doesn't close. *Fix:* unify on one term
  (APEX/AUTONOMY use "principal"; the README uses "operator"), or add a
  half-sentence where the two halves meet — "the operator running the browser at
  rungs 4–5 is the principal whose vault the boundary guards" — so the identity
  the argument leans on is stated, not assumed.

- **R3 [Minor] — the purpose test and the browser-riding rule can disagree on a
  provisioned browser profile; state which wins.** The test classifies by
  *purpose*: a login saved in the rung-4 **dedicated** profile *specifically so
  the agent can use it* is, by the test's own logic, "provisioned … in scope by
  design." But the browser rule is categorical: "may **never** reach for the
  stored credentials that would mint a session." A reader can construct a store
  that the purpose test admits and the browser rule forbids. Operationally there's
  no live contradiction (even in the dedicated profile the agent *rides* the
  session the operator authenticated and never mints), but the doc doesn't say
  that's why. *Fix:* one sentence — a browser's saved-credential store is never
  itself the provisioned path; provisioned *browser* access means the operator
  authenticates and the agent rides the session, so the ride-not-mint line holds
  regardless of which profile it is. That resolves the seam and strengthens the
  test rather than exempting the browser from it.

- **R4 [Low] — the crisp 6-rung table may over-sell the instance's cleanliness on
  rungs 4/5.** The generic ladder presents 4 and 5 as two distinct rungs; the
  built instance serves both with one `browser_fetch_persistent`, split only by
  which profile the operator exposes. This is honestly disclosed in "what lives
  elsewhere," so it is **not a defect** — but a reader hitting the clean table
  first may not carry the caveat down. *Fix (optional):* a parenthetical at the
  ladder — "(a given instance may serve rungs 4–5 with one mechanism; see 'what
  lives elsewhere')" — so the abstraction and its partial realisation travel
  together.

- **R5 [Low] — the headline boundary reads absolute; the grant exception lands
  only later.** Lines 8 ("**never** mint access from the principal's saved
  credentials") and the closing "touching the credentials behind it is the line"
  state the rule at full strength; "The principal can grant across the line"
  appears mid-body. Standard rule-then-exception rhetoric, but the honest-caveat
  bar (APEX) slightly favours signalling the exception at first statement. *Fix:*
  "never mint access from the principal's saved credentials **without an explicit
  grant**" in the intro, or "off-limits **by default**" — matching the phrasing
  the body already uses for the store class.

**Disposition (2026-07-12, session 47 — coordinating session).** All five
findings **[fixed]** in one pass on REACH.md: R1 → "a provisioning registry's
entries" (indefinite, the concrete registry/keychain naming left to the
instance doc); R2 → the operator=principal identity now stated at the join
("the operator running the browser there is the principal whose vault the
boundary guards"); R3 → the seam closed ("A browser's saved-credential store is
never itself the provisioned path … ride-not-mint holds whichever profile it is
— even a dedicated one stood up for the agent's use"); R4 → parenthetical pulled
up beside the ladder ("a given instance may even serve rungs 4 and 5 with one
mechanism … though the rungs stay distinct in principle"); R5 → "without an
explicit grant" added at the intro's first statement of the boundary. Verdict
stands at PASS-WITH-FINDINGS; doctrine ships.

**Close.** The design is sound and ships as doctrine. The ladder is a clean,
honest abstraction of a deliberately partial instance; the join is argued on a
real mechanism; the purpose-of-storage test cuts the credential problem at the
right joint and — the thing the author was guarding — does not outlaw the
estate's own provisioned use. None of R1–R5 is blocking: R1/R2 are
adopter-clarity and terminology, R3 is a genuine but narrow seam that a single
sentence closes, R4/R5 are polish. Fold all five in one pass, or take R1–R3 now
and R4–R5 as polish — either way the verdict stands at PASS-WITH-FINDINGS.
